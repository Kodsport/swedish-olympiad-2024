\problemname{Segmentmonitoring}
\noindent
After successfully conquering all city-states in Kattago, the super-feline kingdom of 
Katen and its leader Kattis want better control over their conquered city-states, especially in Segby. 
The state of Segby consists of a single long line, where there is a house at every integer. 
Recently, rumors of rebellion are circulating, something that will not be tolerated. 
Specifically, they have determined that all suspected houses are located between $x_{min}$ and $x_{max}$, 
meaning the suspected houses are those standing on an integer $x$ such that $x_{min} \leq x \leq x_{max}$.

To avoid a cat-astrophe, Kattis has asked the company Purrfect Segments Inc. to produce various cameras 
that can monitor the suspected segment and the entire street between $x_{min}$ and $x_{max}$ 
(Kattis herself has realized the importance of surveillance after several attempts to attack a well-surveilled wall). 
Purrfect Segments Inc. install their cameras at specific paw-sitions so that each camera can monitor a segment $[a, b]$ to purr-fection, 
meaning the camera monitors all integers $x$ on the number line such that $a \leq x \leq b$. 
The only problem is that the cameras are often vandalized, as they are very similar to speed cameras. 
When a camera is vandalized, it can no longer monitor its segment. 
To keep track of the suspected segment, they will constantly need to install new cameras.

They now seek your help for the next $Q$ days. Initially, there are no cameras monitoring the street at all. 
Every morning, they will check if a camera has been vandalized. 
If so, they will not install a camera that day to avoid detection by the rebels. 
Fortunately, the rebels can vandalize at most $1$ camera per day. 
To plan for the next day, every evening they want to know if they can monitor the suspected segment, and if so, how many cameras are needed. 
Since they want to appear even more efficient than the municipality of Lund, they never want to activate more than two cameras in a day.

For each of the next $Q$ days, can you calculate whether it is possible to use only 1 camera or 2?



\section*{Input}
The first line of the input contains the integers $x_{min}$ and $x_{max}$ ($1 \leq x_{min} < x_{max} \leq 10^9$), 
indicating the segment where the suspected houses are located.

The second line contains the integer $Q$ ($1 \le Q \le 2 \cdot 10^5$), the number of days in the test case.

The following $Q$ lines start either with ``+'' if a camera is to be installed or ``-'' if a camera has been vandalized:
\begin{itemize}
  \item ``+'': The line contains 3 integers, $s$, $a$, and $b$ ($1\le s \le 10^6$, $0\le a < b \le 10^9$), 
  indicating that a camera with serial number $s$ has been installed to monitor the segment $[a,b]$.
  \item ``-'': The line contains the integer $s$, the serial number of the vandalized camera $s$. 
  It is guaranteed that the vandalized camera was installed.
\end{itemize}

All cameras have a unique serial number.

\section*{Output}
Print $Q$ lines, where the $i$-th line is the answer for the $i$-th day:
\begin{itemize}
  \item If it can be monitored with $1$ camera, print $1$.
  \item If it is not possible to monitor with $1$ camera but is possible with $2$, print $2$.
  \item Otherwise, print $-1$.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Points}
Your solution will be tested on several test case groups.
To get the points for a group, it must pass all the test cases in the group.

\noindent
\begin{tabular}{| l | l | p{12cm} |}
  \hline
  \textbf{Group} & \textbf{Point value} & \textbf{Constraints} \\ \hline
  $1$    & $10$       & $Q \le 2$ \\ \hline
  $2$    & $10$       & The answer is either 1 or -1. \\ \hline
  $3$    & $20$       & $Q \le 100$ \\ \hline
  $4$    & $25$       & $Q \le 1000$. \\ \hline
  $5$    & $35$       & No additional constraints. \\ \hline
\end{tabular}

\section*{Explanation of sample 1}
On the first day, camera with serial number ``1'' was installed on the segment $[2,3]$.
There is no way to monitor the entire segment $[2,6]$ by activating any cameras, so the answer for the first day is ``-1''.

On the second day, camera with serial number ``2'' was installed on the segment $[4,6]$.
There is still no way to monitor the entire segment, as it would be possible only if we activate cameras ``1'' and ``2'', leaving the segment between 3 and 4 unmonitored.
Therefore, the answer for the second day is ``-1''.

On the third day, camera with serial number ``2'' was vandalized.
There is still no way to monitor the entire segment $[2,6]$, so the answer for the third day is ``-1''.

On the fourth day, camera with serial number ``3'' was installed.
By activating cameras ``1'' and ``3'' the segment $[2,7]$ is monitored, including the suspected segment $[2,6]$.
Since there is no way to monitor $[2,6]$ by activating only 1 camera, the answer for the fourth day is ``2''.


\section*{Explanation of sample 3}
On the first day, camera with serial number ``123'' was installed on the segment $[10,15]$.
There is no way to monitor the entire segment $[10,20]$ by activating any cameras, so the answer for the first day is ``-1''.

On the second day, camera with serial number ``234'' was installed.
By activating cameras ``123'' and ``234,'' the entire secret segment $[10,20]$ is monitored.
Therefore, the answer for the second day is ``2''.

On the third day, camera with serial number ``345'' was installed.
By activating only camera ``345,'' the entire secret segment $[10,20]$ is monitored.
Therefore, the answer for the third day is ``1''.
