\problemname{Avalanche}

\noindent
After Joshua saw through Alexander's attempts to fake pictures of snowflakes\footnote{See https://open.kattis.com/problems/falskflinga}, 
Alexander has become obsessed with snow, and very angry. 
He has come up with an evil master plan with unclear motives, 
where the first step was to invite Joshua on a skiing trip. 
Together, they are now in a large ski system with $N$ ski villages numbered from $1$ to $N$. 
The ski villages are connected by $N-1$ one-way ski slopes. 
At the top of the ski system is ski village $1$, and from there, 
one can reach all other villages by skiing down the slopes. 
For each other ski village, there is exactly one slope leading to the village.

Alexander now wants to play a ``little'' prank. 
He wants to start an avalanche in the ski system. 
He plans to do this by standing in any ski village and screaming very loudly. 
The snow in this village will then spread down along all slopes originating from the village. 
Then the snow will spread further downward in all directions as far as possible. 
The \emph{damage} of the avalanche is the number of ski villages affected by it.

Joshua, noticing that Alexander does not seem to be in his right mind, 
has seen through Alexander's evil master plan. 
Joshua is very handy and can build walls in certain ski villages. 
A wall in a specific ski village prevents the avalanche from spreading to that village. 
Alexander also cannot start the avalanche from a walled ski village.

But time is running out, 
and since Joshua must first and foremost warn all other skiers about what is about to happen, 
he can only build walls in $K$ different ski villages. 
Joshua does not know in which ski village Alexander will stand and scream, 
but he wants to minimize the number of ski villages affected by avalanches.

First, Joshua will place the $K$ walls so that the maximum damage Alexander can cause is minimized. 
Then Alexander will stand and scream in the node that causes the maximum damage. 
Your task is to calculate the extent of the damage, i.e., the number of ski villages affected by the avalanche.

\section*{Input}
The first line of input has the two numbers $N$ and $K$ ($1 \le K < N \le 10^5$),
the number of ski villages in the ski system, and the number of walls Joshua can place.

Then there is a row containing the $N-1$ numbers $a_1, a_2, ..., a_{N-1}$, where $1 \le a_i \le i$.
For each $i = 1, 2, ..., N-1$, there is a ski slope from the ski village with number $a_i$ to the ski village with number $i+1$.

\section*{Output}
Output a single number: The maximum damage Alexander can cause, given that Joshua places the walls optimally.

\section*{Points}
Your solution will be tested on several test case groups.
To get the points for a group, it must pass all the test cases in the group.
